# References

The Cockroach Janta Party movement of May to July 2026 did not emerge in a vacuum. It stood on decades of Indian public life: prior legislation, prior court cases, prior movements, prior hunger strikes, prior grief. The list below points to what shaped it, what it built on, and what it should be read alongside.

If you find a source that anchors a specific event, quote, or emotional beat in the timeline, open a pull request.

## Legal and constitutional context

- **Section 69A of the Information Technology Act, 2000.** The provision the Union Government invoked on May 21, 2026 to block CJP's X account. Repeatedly used in prior years for platform-level blocking, and the exact statute the Delhi High Court pushed back against in the July 11 restoration ruling.
- **Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015).** Supreme Court judgment that struck down Section 66A of the IT Act and clarified the procedural limits of the Government's Section 69A blocking powers. Foundational precedent for the July 11 Delhi HC ruling in this timeline.
- **X Corp. v. Union of India (Karnataka High Court, 2023 onwards).** Earlier challenge by X to Section 69A blocking orders as arbitrary and disproportionate. Established the legal pattern that CJP's counsel drew on.

## Activists and prior movements the CJP stood on the shoulders of

- **Sonam Wangchuk.** Ladakhi engineer, educator, and climate activist whose 21-day fast in March 2024 for Ladakh's Sixth Schedule protection reintroduced sustained hunger-strike protest to Indian public consciousness. Detained at Jantar Mantar on July 18, 2026 while on day 21 of a solidarity fast for the NEET aspirants.
- **The NEET-UG 2024 paper leak protests.** The 2024 outrage of medical aspirants and their families following confirmed leaks and multiple cancellations. The direct precursor to the 2026 leak and to the wave of aspirant grief the CJP amplified.

## Historical protest movements this archive is in conversation with

- **The 2020 to 2021 farmer protests (Singhu, Tikri, Ghazipur).** The template for sustained, cross-generational, camp-based Indian protest. The mechanics of the Jantar Mantar campsite in June and July 2026 draw heavily from this.
- **The anti-CAA protests (2019 to 2020).** Youth-led, university-anchored, deeply symbolic. Read Shaheen Bagh's tactics into every candlelight vigil scene in this archive.
- **The JNU and Jamia student movements (2019 to present).** The immediate lineage of the queer, trans, and student solidarity documented in the "They Showed Up and Showed Out" context card.

## Conceptual references

- **The Streisand Effect.** The phenomenon in which an attempt to suppress information amplifies its reach. Coined in 2003 after Barbra Streisand sued to remove aerial photos of her home from public archives. What played out on May 22, 2026, when the CJP's Instagram passed 16.4 million followers within twenty-four hours of the X ban.
- **Satyagraha as method.** Non-violent resistance as articulated by M. K. Gandhi and adapted through every Indian mass movement since. The "An All-Age Satyagraha" context card names this lineage explicitly.
