# References

This is a fictional archive of a protest movement that did not literally happen. Every strand of it, though, is anchored in real Indian public life: real legislation, real courtrooms, real streets, real hunger strikes, real grief. The list below points to what the fiction draws from.

If you find a source that anchors a specific event, quote, or emotional beat in the timeline, open a pull request.

## Real legal and constitutional context

- **Section 69A of the Information Technology Act, 2000.** The provision the fictional Union Government uses to block CJP's X account. Used repeatedly in real life for platform-level blocking of accounts and posts.
- **Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015).** Supreme Court judgment that struck down Section 66A of the IT Act and clarified the procedural limits of the Government's Section 69A blocking powers. Foundational reading on Indian free-speech jurisprudence.
- **X Corp. v. Union of India (Karnataka High Court, 2023 onwards).** Real challenge by X to blocking orders as arbitrary and disproportionate. Directly parallels the fictional July 11 Delhi HC ruling in this timeline.

## Real activists whose spirit informs the movement

- **Sonam Wangchuk.** Ladakhi engineer, educator, and climate activist. His real 21-day fast in March 2024 for Ladakh's Sixth Schedule protection is the direct inspiration for his mention in the July 18 tab.
- **The NEET-UG 2024 paper leak protests.** The real nationwide outrage of medical aspirants and their families following confirmed leaks and multiple cancellations. The fictional 21 martyrs echo real reports of aspirant suicides tied to exam-pressure and repeat-exam trauma.

## Historical protest movements this archive is in conversation with

- **The 2020 to 2021 farmer protests (Singhu, Tikri, Ghazipur).** The template for sustained, cross-generational, camp-based Indian protest. The mechanics of the fictional Jantar Mantar campsite draw heavily from this.
- **The anti-CAA protests (2019 to 2020).** Youth-led, university-anchored, deeply symbolic. Read Shaheen Bagh's tactics into every candlelight vigil scene in this archive.
- **The JNU and Jamia student movements (2019 to present).** For the tone of the queer, trans, and Left-adjacent student solidarity documented in the "They Showed Up and Showed Out" context card.

## Conceptual references

- **The Streisand Effect.** The phenomenon in which an attempt to suppress information amplifies its reach. Coined in 2003 after Barbra Streisand sued to remove aerial photos of her home from public archives. The fictional May 22 tab turns on this exact dynamic.
- **Satyagraha as method.** Non-violent resistance as articulated by M. K. Gandhi and adapted through every Indian mass movement since. The "An All-Age Satyagraha" context card names this lineage explicitly.
